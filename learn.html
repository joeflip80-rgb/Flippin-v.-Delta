What is a peptide?

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins — linked together by peptide bonds. Think of amino acids as beads and a peptide as a short string of them. Your body makes thousands of peptides naturally, where they act as signaling molecules: tiny messengers that tell cells what to do, from regulating blood sugar to triggering tissue repair.

Because they're signaling molecules, researchers are interested in whether synthetic versions can be used as tools to study — or potentially influence — specific biological pathways.

Peptides vs. proteins

The difference is mostly size. There's no universal cut-off, but a common rule of thumb:

MoleculeTypical lengthExample
Peptide2–50 amino acidsInsulin's individual chains, oxytocin
Polypeptide~50–100 amino acidsLonger signaling molecules
Protein100+ amino acids, foldedHemoglobin, antibodies

Smaller size is partly why peptides are studied so widely: they can be specific to a single receptor or pathway, and they're often easier to synthesize than full proteins.

Common categories you'll encounter

  • Growth hormone secretagogues — studied for stimulating the body's own growth-hormone release (e.g., Ipamorelin, CJC-1295).
  • Metabolic / incretin peptides — researched for glucose and appetite regulation (e.g., GLP-1 analogs).
  • Recovery / repair peptides — investigated in tissue-healing models (e.g., BPC-157, TB-500).
  • Cosmetic peptides — used in skin research for collagen and signaling (e.g., GHK-Cu).
  • Nootropic peptides — explored for cognition in animal studies (e.g., Semax, Selank).

Key terminology, decoded

TermWhat it means
LyophilizedFreeze-dried into a powder for stability; usually reconstituted with liquid before study.
ReconstitutionAdding a measured liquid (often bacteriostatic water) to dissolve a powdered peptide.
Bacteriostatic waterSterile water with a small amount of preservative to inhibit bacterial growth.
Half-lifeHow long it takes for half the substance to clear — a clue to how long it stays active.
SecretagogueA substance that prompts the body to secrete something (like growth hormone).
BioavailabilityHow much of a dose actually reaches circulation in active form.
mcg vs mg1 milligram (mg) = 1,000 micrograms (mcg). Doses are often tiny, so mcg is common.

How to read peptide research critically

The single most useful habit: ask "studied in what?" A result in a petri dish or a mouse is a long way from a proven human benefit.
  • Check the model. In vitro (cells), in vivo animal, and human clinical trials carry very different weight.
  • Watch the wording. "May," "associated with," and "in preclinical models" signal early, uncertain evidence.
  • Look for sample size and controls. Small, uncontrolled studies are hypotheses, not conclusions.
  • Separate marketing from science. Sellers benefit from optimistic framing; primary literature and reviews are more reliable.

Safety, regulation & legality

This is the part that's easy to skip and most important. Many peptides discussed online are research chemicals not approved for human use. Their purity, sterility, and long-term effects are often poorly characterized, and legal status varies widely between countries and even regions.

Bottom line: Use this site to understand peptides, not as a protocol. Decisions about your health belong with a qualified, licensed professional who knows your situation.
